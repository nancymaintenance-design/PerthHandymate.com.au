Door, window and timber repairs in Swanbourne

Swanbourne enquiries can cover a sticking entrance door, a window that will not latch, torn screen mesh or damaged timber finishes. Start with the affected item rather than a broad request to refresh the whole house. Describe whether the fault is constant or changes after rain, which room or side of the property is affected and whether anyone can use the opening safely. These observations help Ellis assess the repair without assuming the cause.

Door, window and screen repairs in Swanbourne

Keep separate notes for the door leaf, frame, latch and any screen fitted beside it. A catching door and a damaged mesh panel may need different parts and preparation. For a window, explain whether the problem is movement, locking or visible damage. Do not force a stuck opening or touch unstable glass. Tell the team if a fixture is shared with another property or if access requires an owner or building manager to be present.

Timber finishes, wall patches and cabinet hardware

A loose cabinet hinge should be described alongside the condition of the panel and fixing holes. A damaged wall patch may also need paint preparation, but colour matching depends on the existing finish. If an exterior timber surface is soft, split or flaking, explain what is visible from a safe position. Ellis checks the material condition and repair boundary before discussing replacement, preparation or painting. An enquiry does not establish that a particular treatment will suit the surface.

Agree the repair boundary and property access

Existing product details and photos are useful if already available, but neither is required to start a request. You can describe a fault in plain language and ask for an on-site assessment. Avoid removing fittings, moving heavy furniture or inspecting at height to gather information. Sticking-door repairs

Describe where the door catches, whether the latch closes and any visible frame damage. Adjustment, hardware and replacement needs are checked before the repair is agreed.

Window opening and latch repairs

Tell us whether opening, closing or locking is affected. Keep clear of loose glass; note the room and the condition of the frame and hardware from a safe position.

Loose hinges and drawer repairs

Identify the cupboard or wardrobe, the number of affected doors or drawers and whether a hinge, runner or panel is loose. Parts and fixing surfaces need assessment.

Blocked and leaking gutter repairs

Report overflowing sections, leaks or visible damage from ground level. Do not climb to inspect; roof access, debris removal and the repair scope are checked separately.

Wall and ceiling patching

Describe the size and location of the damaged area and whether moisture is still present. The cause, surface preparation and finishing work must be understood before patching.

Torn flyscreen and mesh repairs

Tell us whether the screen is on a door or window and whether the mesh, frame or hardware is damaged. Confirm the required material and fit after assessment.
