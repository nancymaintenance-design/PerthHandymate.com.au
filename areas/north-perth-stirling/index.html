Established homes, townhouses and managed properties

Door, cabinet and plaster repairs on your home repair list

Loose fittings and damaged finishes: define the repair list

For an established home, a loose hinge or damaged timber edge needs more information than the room name. Note whether the screw fixing, hinge, door edge or frame appears affected. If the problem has returned after an earlier repair, mention that history. Ellis checks the surrounding material before agreeing a repair, because replacing hardware alone may not address a damaged fixing surface.

Townhouse repairs can involve both private rooms and access through a shared driveway or entry. Describe where the work sits within the property and whether gates, stairs or stored items affect access. Keep repair decisions separate from assumptions about shared ownership. Where another person manages the property, explain who arranges entry and who is authorised to accept the written quote.

Door hardware, wall patches and paint preparation

For a door that will not close, use door repairs and installation and describe whether adjustment, frame damage or replacement is your concern. Include existing handles and locks that you want retained. For cupboard doors or drawers, cabinet repairs and fitted storage explains the joinery work that can be assessed. Tell us whether the request is a small fitting repair or a change to the storage itself.

When damaged trim meets a marked wall, list the timber work and finish separately. wall and ceiling patching can be discussed alongside the existing carpentry service below. Give each item a location and preferred outcome, such as retaining the original trim or preparing a patch for painting. This is more useful than calling the whole list a renovation.

Agree the repair scope and occupied-room access

For tenant-occupied properties, provide the access contact, any agreed appointment window and the repair items already authorised. If a new issue is found during assessment, it can be described separately for approval. Safely taken photos of hinges, frames or damaged finishes are useful if available, but you can start with a written description. Ellis confirms the repair method, material requirements and quote after checking the property rather than assuming the same solution suits every established home or townhouse.

Door, cupboard and trim repairs

If a door catches, a cupboard hinge pulls out or timber trim is damaged, describe the affected items as one repair list. We assess the fittings and surrounding material before agreeing work. For a managed property, include tenant access and the person who can approve the quote.

Choose the repair you need

Sticking-door repairs

Describe where the door catches, whether the latch closes and any visible frame damage. Adjustment, hardware and replacement needs are checked before the repair is agreed.

Window opening and latch repairs

Tell us whether opening, closing or locking is affected. Keep clear of loose glass; note the room and the condition of the frame and hardware from a safe position.

Loose hinges and drawer repairs

Identify the cupboard or wardrobe, the number of affected doors or drawers and whether a hinge, runner or panel is loose. Parts and fixing surfaces need assessment.

Blocked and leaking gutter repairs

Report overflowing sections, leaks or visible damage from ground level. Do not climb to inspect; roof access, debris removal and the repair scope are checked separately.

Wall and ceiling patching

Describe the size and location of the damaged area and whether moisture is still present. The cause, surface preparation and finishing work must be understood before patching.

Torn flyscreen and mesh repairs

Tell us whether the screen is on a door or window and whether the mesh, frame or hardware is damaged. Confirm the required material and fit after assessment.
