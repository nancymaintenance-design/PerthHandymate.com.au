Central apartments and inner-suburb homes

Sticking doors, cabinet fittings and wall patches in inner-suburb homes

Door and window repairs: describe the affected room

A central apartment enquiry benefits from two short lists: what needs attention inside the home, and how the team can reach it. Identify the room and affected item for each repair, then add the floor, lift arrangements and available parking if known. Explain whether the damaged surface or fitting is within your home or in a shared area. This helps Ellis discuss the assessment with the right person before materials or attendance are agreed.

For an established inner-suburb house, describe the same practical details without assuming an apartment process applies. A narrow entry, stairs or furniture beside the repair may matter more than the street address alone. You do not need to move heavy items or take measurements at height. Tell us what you can see and which rooms are occupied so the visit can be planned around the household.

Cabinet, screen and paint repairs by item

For a door that catches, note whether it rubs at the top, side or floor and whether the frame or latch has visible damage. For a window, describe whether opening, closing or locking is the problem. Open window repairs for hardware and frame enquiries. A torn mesh panel belongs under flyscreen repairs; include whether it is a door or window screen.

If a wall patch also needs colour matching, discuss painting and touch-ups alongside the patching request. Existing paint information can help, but an invisible match is not assumed. List loose cabinet fittings separately from wall damage so preparation, fitting and finishing can be identified in the quote.

Shared doors and surfaces: confirm repair permission

For managed apartments, tell Ellis who can approve work and how building-management permission is arranged where relevant. An access contact and a quote approver may be different people. Keep both roles clear, especially for shared doors or surfaces. Ellis assesses the items, confirms the practical work scope and provides a written quote; a grouped request does not mean every repair uses the same method or can be completed in one visit.

Apartment and inner-suburb repair lists

For an apartment repair, tell us about building access, lifts, parking and the person who approves work. Loose cabinet hardware, wall patches and window fittings can be assessed together. For shared areas, confirm building-management permission before arranging the visit.

Sticking-door repairs in Perth

Describe where the door catches, whether the latch closes and any visible frame damage. Adjustment, hardware and replacement needs are checked before the repair is agreed.

Window opening and latch repairs in Northbridge

Tell us whether opening, closing or locking is affected. Keep clear of loose glass; note the room and the condition of the frame and hardware from a safe position.

Loose hinges and drawer repairs in West Perth

Identify the cupboard or wardrobe, the number of affected doors or drawers and whether a hinge, runner or panel is loose. Parts and fixing surfaces need assessment.

Blocked and leaking gutter repairs in East Perth

Report overflowing sections, leaks or visible damage from ground level. Do not climb to inspect; roof access, debris removal and the repair scope are checked separately.

Wall and ceiling patching in Highgate

Describe the size and location of the damaged area and whether moisture is still present. The cause, surface preparation and finishing work must be understood before patching.

Torn flyscreen and mesh repairs in Mount Lawley

Tell us whether the screen is on a door or window and whether the mesh, frame or hardware is damaged. Confirm the required material and fit after assessment.
