Assessment details for timber fence & gate repairs

Local fence damage and a leaning fence line

Timber fence repair can replace suitable damaged palings or a local panel after posts, rails and connections are checked. A leaning fence requires the support and ground condition assessed, not only new surface boards.

A single-panel replacement may retain sound surrounding structure. Ellis checks post condition, access, boundary approval and underground services before digging or replacement. Old unidentified fibre-cement material needs separate assessment before disturbance.

Wind-damaged palings and a fence leaning beside a driveway need different repair plans. Keep clear of unstable sections, describe the location and confirm who can authorise boundary work. Do not remove old sheets to inspect concealed posts.

Why leaning returns

A repaired fence may lean again if posts, footings, rails or ground movement remain unresolved. Replacing palings alone does not strengthen a failing support. The whole affected section needs assessment before another local repair is chosen.

Old fibre-cement material

Old unidentified fibre-cement fencing may contain asbestos. Material must be assessed before disturbance; do not cut, drill or remove sheets to investigate. See WorkSafe asbestos guidance. Ellis identifies the appropriate assessment and work requirements separately from ordinary timber repairs.

A sagging side gate and its supporting posts

Ordinary timber gate repairs can address suitable hinges, latch alignment and a gate that drags. The gate, hinge fixings and supporting post need checks together before adjustment is agreed.

A sagging gate may be repairable without replacing the whole unit where its timber and support remain sound. Worn hardware, decay or a moving post can change that decision. Automated, security and pool-barrier gates have separate requirements.

A side gate that will not latch after rain or allows a dog to escape needs the current gap and support condition assessed. Do not rely on an unstable gate for containment while waiting for the repair; confirm boundary approval and access.

Repeated gate drop

A gate can drop when hinge fixings loosen, timber decays, the leaf distorts or the supporting post moves. Latch adjustment alone may hide the symptom temporarily. The gate and its supports need assessment before a durable repair is selected.

Pool gates are safety equipment

A pool gate forms part of a safety barrier and needs its closing, latching and complete barrier requirements assessed. Ordinary garden-gate adjustment does not establish compliance. Ellis treats pool-barrier repairs as a separate specialist scope.

Timber Fencing in Perth: Common Questions

Can loose or damaged timber palings be replaced individually?

Individual boards can form part of the repair scope when the posts and rails remain sound. Rot or storm damage in the supporting sections may require more than new palings.

Can a sagging timber side gate be repaired without replacement?

The hinges, gate frame, post and latch alignment need checking first. Adjustment may suit sound components, while decay or movement can require replacement of the affected support or gate parts.

Detailed timber fence & gate repairs questions

Why does a repaired fence keep leaning?

Post or footing deterioration, damaged connections or ground movement can remain after surface repairs. Ellis assesses the affected supports, surroundings and any retaining function before choosing local work or replacement. New palings alone may not address the cause.

What should happen before repairing an old fibre-cement fence?

Leave unidentified material undisturbed and arrange appropriate assessment before repair or removal. Suspected asbestos is not an ordinary timber-fence task. See WorkSafe asbestos guidance; do not cut, drill or pressure clean suspect sheets.

Why does a gate keep dropping on one side?

Hinges, fixing material, leaf condition or a moving post can cause repeated drop. Ellis checks the gate and support together; moving the latch alone may not resolve the cause.

Are pool-gate repairs different from ordinary gate repairs?

Yes. The gate is part of a safety barrier, with closing, latching and barrier requirements to assess. Ellis confirms the appropriate pool-fence scope and personnel. An ordinary hinge adjustment alone does not establish compliance.
