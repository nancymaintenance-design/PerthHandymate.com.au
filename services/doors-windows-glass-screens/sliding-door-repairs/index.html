Rollers, tracks and alignment

Dragging sliders and damaged tracks

External sliding-door repair concerns the glass panel, rollers, track and compatible operating hardware. A heavy patio door that drags needs assessment of both the moving panel and the surface it runs on.

Roller replacement may retain a serviceable door, but panel condition, track wear, safe handling and compatible parts determine the scope and cost. Ellis distinguishes adjustment, hardware replacement and track work in the written quote.

For a jammed apartment balcony door, explain whether the glass panel or insect screen is stuck and confirm access and property approval. Do not lift a heavy glass panel out to photograph its rollers; photos are optional.

Several causes of drag

Worn rollers, debris, distorted tracks and poor alignment can all make a slider hard to move. More than one fault may be present. Repeatedly forcing the panel can worsen damage; the panel and track need assessment together.

Retain or replace the track

A track does not always need complete replacement. Its shape, wear, damage and compatibility with the panel determine whether adjustment or a local repair is practical. New rollers will not correct a seriously damaged running surface.

Patio glass doors and wardrobe sliders

Exterior patio sliders and interior wardrobe doors use different tracks, supports and fittings. For a wardrobe fault, describe the door material and whether it has a mirror; see our cupboard and wardrobe repair service. We assess each system rather than assuming patio-door parts will fit.

Heavy, loose or cracked glass panels need a handling assessment before removal. Avoid forcing an unstable door or lifting the panel yourself. Ellis confirms suitable personnel, access and the panel-handling method. Any glass replacement is identified as a distinct part of the scope.

Repair options and quotes

We first check how the panel moves and closes, along with the rollers, running surface and frame. We then explain whether adjustment, compatible replacement parts or track work is needed and confirm the written quote before the repair.

A local repair may be appropriate when the frame and panel remain serviceable and compatible parts can be obtained. Extensive frame damage, unavailable hardware or damaged glass can change the options. We explain the findings and compare the proposed repair with any door installation or replacement work before you approve the quote.

Let us know your suburb, the number of doors, the fault and any access restrictions. Photos of the whole door or existing hardware are optional; an on-site assessment is available. Parts, removal, handling and finishing requirements affect the quote.

Sliding door repair FAQs

Can you replace sliding-door rollers without replacing the door?

Often a serviceable panel can retain its frame and receive compatible rollers. Ellis checks the track, panel and hardware first, then confirms parts and a written quote. A damaged track or frame may require additional work.

Do you repair wardrobe sliding doors as well as patio sliders?

Tell us which type is affected. Wardrobe sliders, including mirrored panels, need their own hardware and handling assessment. We confirm whether the repair is suitable rather than treating every slider as the same system.

What should I prepare before an assessment?

A short fault description, your suburb and access details are enough to start. Photos and measurements are optional. Tell us if the glass is cracked, the panel is loose or the door cannot be secured.

Why is a sliding door difficult to move?

Rollers, track condition, debris or alignment can restrict travel. Ellis checks these together and identifies compatible parts. Avoid forcing a jammed or unstable panel or removing glass to inspect it yourself.

Does a damaged sliding-door track always need replacement?

No. Ellis checks the extent of damage and the panel system before deciding whether a local repair or replacement is suitable. Roller condition and the running surface are assessed together rather than treating one part in isolation.
