Tiling & Tile Repairs

Shower regrouting: tile condition and leak causes

Shower regrouting renews joints between suitable tiles. Ellis first checks for loose tiles, movement, damaged edges and signs of continuing water entry. A weak substrate or repeated moisture problem needs investigation before fresh grout covers it. Tell us where grout is missing and whether the shower still leaks after earlier repairs.

The quote identifies the repair area, grout removal, soundness of retained tiles, replacement-tile availability and any substrate work. Matching tile or grout appearance is assessed rather than promised. See the existing bathroom surface repair example for tile and finish work; it does not establish the cause of your leak.

Shower resealing: joints and silicone

Shower resealing concerns the visible flexible joints, including worn silicone at suitable junctions. The agreed process covers removal of failed sealant, cleaning and preparation of the joint, a compatible replacement and the product's return-to-use instructions. Tell Ellis about previous sealants and recurring discolouration, gaps or leaks.

Will fresh silicone stop every shower leak?

No. Loose tiles, persistent damp or repeated failure can involve plumbing, movement or the membrane beneath the finish. Surface resealing is not membrane replacement or a guaranteed leak cure. Use leaking-shower assessment if the source is unclear, and read how silicone, grout and waterproofing differ before agreeing cosmetic work.

What we check on site

Grout, silicone and the cause of a shower leak

Shower resealing and regrouting address different surface joints. Split silicone, missing grout or blackened sealant need the joint condition and moisture history assessed before a repair is selected.

Regrouting cost depends on area, joint condition, preparation and access. Resealing does not fix every shower leak: plumbing, movement or membrane failure may need separate investigation. Ellis identifies the accepted surface repair and any wider work in the quote.

For black silicone around a shower base or grout falling out between tiles, mention previous repairs and water appearing outside the shower. A recurring leak should be described as a leak, not only as a request for new silicone.

Different joint materials

Grout fills the joints between tiles; silicone provides a flexible seal at suitable junctions or movement joints. The materials have different roles and are not interchangeable in every location. Ellis checks the joint and cause of deterioration before specifying replacement.

Surface resealing and membrane work

Resealing renews visible flexible joints. Waterproofing repair addresses the membrane and junctions beneath or behind finishes and can require tile removal and substrate repair. Renewing surface silicone does not establish that the underlying waterproofing is sound.

Damaged tiles and underlying problems

Local tile repair can address a chipped or cracked floor tile, a loose splashback tile or a few damaged joints. Matching tile availability and the condition of the backing determine whether a small replacement is practical.

Replacing one tile may be possible where surrounding tiles and substrate remain suitable. Ellis checks movement, moisture and adhesive condition before quoting; lifting several tiles or repairing waterproofing can change the scope.

A chipped kitchen floor tile differs from a loose tile behind a sink. Describe impact, water entry, hollow areas and repeat cracking. Do not prise off a loose tile to prove the fault before an assessment.

A hollow sound is a clue

A hollow-sounding tile may have voids or areas of poor bond, but sound alone does not establish the extent or cause of failure. Movement, cracking, moisture and substrate condition need assessment alongside the surface symptom.

Cracks beneath the finish

Cracked tiles can result from impact, movement, inadequate support or moisture-related problems below the finish. Repeated cracks or several affected tiles need the cause checked before replacement. A new tile alone does not repair an unstable backing.

Tiling: Common Questions

Can just one cracked kitchen floor tile be replaced?

A localised replacement may be possible if a compatible tile is available and the surrounding surface is sound. We assess the damaged tile and substrate before confirming the scope. Repeated cracking or loose tiles may point to a larger problem underneath.

Can you renew grout that is falling out between shower tiles?

Yes, after checking the underlying condition. Loose tiles, movement and persistent moisture need assessment before renewing grout or silicone. Regrouting does not replace a failed membrane; see the difference between surface resealing and waterproofing when a shower keeps leaking.

What affects the cost of a small tiling or regrouting job?

The area, tile size, layout, matching material and preparation needed affect the quote. Access and the condition of the existing surface also matter. Tell us whether tiles are supplied and about any known moisture or movement issue.

What is the difference between grout and silicone?

Grout fills tile joints, while silicone provides a flexible seal at suitable junctions. Their roles differ. Ellis assesses the joint and moisture history before agreeing which material and preparation are appropriate.

How does shower resealing differ from waterproofing?

Resealing treats visible surface joints. Waterproofing concerns the membrane and its junctions beneath the finishes. Persistent leakage needs diagnosis; tile removal and substrate work may be necessary and are separate from a silicone replacement.

Why can tiles sound hollow?

Voids or reduced bonding can change the sound, but tapping alone does not diagnose the repair. Ellis checks visible damage, movement, moisture and the backing condition before agreeing local replacement or further investigation.

Can cracked tiles indicate an underlying problem?

Yes. Movement, support or moisture can contribute as well as impact. Ellis checks the pattern and history before choosing replacement. Recurring cracks may need substrate or waterproofing investigation rather than another surface repair.
