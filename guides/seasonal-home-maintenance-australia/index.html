Gutters Overflowing after Rain? Seasonal Home Maintenance in Perth

Updated 2026-10-14

After rain, a gutter overflows again, a gate drags or timber near a window looks different. A seasonal checklist is useful only if it records those changes and asks whether ordinary cleaning or adjustment is enough. Repeating the same maintenance task may leave a drainage, support or moisture problem unresolved.

What is included in property maintenance, and how often should minor home maintenance be checked? This guide uses the condition of the property, recent weather and previous repair history to plan observations and follow-up. It explains the boundary between routine upkeep and a fault needing its own assessment, without asking you to climb or dismantle anything.

Record what changed after the weather changed

From safe ground, note where overflow is visible and whether it occurs at the same location as before. Record damp marks, loose fittings or changes in how gates and windows move. You do not need to establish whether a gutter is blocked or incorrectly draining. That distinction belongs in the assessment, particularly when overflow continues after an earlier cleaning.

Use earlier notes to distinguish a new fault from an unresolved one. A date, location and short observation can be enough: water at the same corner after rain tells a clearer story than simply requesting another gutter clean. Do not access a roof, test an unstable structure or disturb older materials to collect better evidence.

Turn a checklist into separate maintenance decisions

Routine cleaning, surface treatment and repair of damaged material need different scopes. Deck oil does not restore a weakened board or support, and repainting a swollen frame does not identify the moisture path. Ask the assessment to distinguish work that maintains a serviceable item from work that repairs a failed component or needs specialist investigation.

Set the next review around the property condition, weather exposure and advice for the actual materials. There is no single calendar interval in this guide that makes every home safe. Keep a record of completed work, outstanding concerns and any access restrictions. An owner away from the property should identify the local access contact and approval path before arranging recurring maintenance.

Preventative maintenance for local conditions

Australia does not have one maintenance calendar. Wet seasons, bushfire seasons, coastal exposure, frost, heat and storm patterns vary widely. Use local authority guidance and the property’s history to set timing. A useful plan combines regular visual checks with inspections by qualified people where access, safety or regulated systems are involved.

Before a locally wet or stormy period, look from safe ground for loose exterior elements, damaged seals, overflowing drainage signs and vegetation affecting access. Clear only what you can reach safely. Roofs, high gutters, power lines and unstable trees require appropriate expertise and equipment.

Routine Property Repairs: Moisture & Outdoor Wear

Ahead of hotter or colder months, check doors and windows for obvious gaps, inspect accessible flyscreens, and note ventilation or condensation problems. Heating, cooling, electrical and gas equipment should be maintained according to manufacturer instructions and by appropriately authorised people where required. Never dismantle equipment for a seasonal check.

Outdoor timber, paint, paving, gates and fences can show early wear before failure. Record lifting surfaces, loose fixings, peeling coatings or drainage changes. In gardens, keep access paths visible and identify irrigation leaks or trip hazards. A cleaned gutter that still overflows needs the drainage concern assessed; oiling a deck does not address damaged boards or supports. A dropping gate or leaning fence also needs its supports considered. Avoid pressure cleaning fragile or unidentified older materials. Tree work, hazardous materials and structural damage require their own assessment rather than an ordinary garden tidy-up.

Create Your Seasonal Home Maintenance To-do List

Keep a dated list, with optional safe photos, and divide it into safety, water or weather exposure, essential function and planned presentation work. Note previous repair dates and known materials. This record helps explain whether an issue is new, recurring or stable and makes it easier to group compatible tasks.

Set reminders around local conditions rather than fixed national dates. After severe weather, note changes from safe ground and update the list when materials or access change.

Torn Flyscreen Mesh & Flyscreen Remeshing

Can I replace just the mesh in my flyscreen frame?

Yes, where the frame and corners are sound, replacing mesh may be appropriate without replacing the frame. Ellis checks the mesh, frame, spline and fit before confirming the work. An ordinary flyscreen does not provide the protection of a security screen.

Can you quote for cat-damaged flyscreens before a rental inspection?

Yes. Count the damaged screens and tell us whether the frame is bent or only the mesh is torn. For a rental, confirm who can approve the work and provide access. We assess replacement mesh and fitting; completion timing is confirmed with you, not assumed from the inspection date.

Can one damaged section of skirting board be replaced?

Sagging Clothesline Wire & Folding Clothesline Repairs

Can a broken clothesline be repaired rather than replaced?

Can you assess a wall-mounted clothesline coming loose from brickwork?

Yes. Stop using an unstable clothesline and explain which brackets have moved. We inspect the wall, anchors, frame and product requirements before agreeing repairs. Retensioning the line alone does not address a failing wall fixing.

Can you replace a damaged laminate board or vinyl plank?

Yes, where the flooring system and available replacement material allow a local repair. Click-lock boards and glued flooring need different access and preparation. Ellis checks how much surrounding flooring must be lifted and whether spare matching material is available before quoting.

What needs checking before a letterbox post is installed?

The proposed location, underground services, boundaries, access and product support requirements need checking before digging. Describe the position and any known service locations. Ellis confirms the work plan after assessment rather than relying on appearance.

Why do outdoor wall fixings loosen over time?

Exposure, corrosion, movement or damaged wall material can weaken the fixing. Ellis checks the product and supporting surface together. Retightening a failed fixing may not address the cause.

Why is laminate lifting at its edges?

Moisture, movement restriction, joint damage or an uneven backing can contribute. Ellis checks the system and fault history before choosing a local repair. A new trim or heavier fixing will not necessarily resolve the underlying problem.

Should moisture damage be addressed before floorboard replacement?

Yes. Ellis assesses the moisture source and substrate condition before reinstatement is agreed. Drying, other trade work or backing repairs may be needed. Replacing the visible board alone does not establish that the cause is resolved.

Can pressure cleaning damage pavers or grout?

Yes. Weak jointing and vulnerable finishes can be damaged by an unsuitable method. Ellis assesses the surface, coatings, joints and drainage first. Paving repairs may need a separate scope before cleaning is suitable.

What to take from this guide

Routine upkeep and repair of an underlying fault are different decisions, and inspection timing depends on the property and conditions.
