Shower Silicone, Grout & Waterproofing: The Difference

A leaking shower does not always need the same repair. Surface silicone, grout, plumbing and the waterproofing beneath tiles perform different roles; the symptoms should guide assessment rather than a single advertised treatment.

Contact Ellis Services Group to arrange an on-site assessment in Perth, identify the cause and confirm the repair plan and quote.

Updated 2026-10-10

Before attempting repairs

This guide helps you describe a fault and plan an assessment. It does not diagnose your property or replace task-specific safety and trade requirements.

Split shower silicone and surface joints

Silicone is commonly used at movement joints and changes of plane in a tiled wet area. A split or deteriorated joint may need surface resealing, but its appearance does not establish the condition of the system behind the tiles. Describe staining, gaps and whether the shower leaks during use. New silicone should not be presented as a guaranteed cure for a leak whose source has not been identified.

Missing tile grout and loose tiles

Grout fills joints between tiles, while the underlying waterproofing is a separate part of the construction. Missing grout, loose tiles or recurring cracking may need more investigation than refilling visible gaps. Note whether tiles have moved and whether damp appears outside the shower. Tell Ellis about previous regrouting or resealing and whether it changed the symptoms. Movement and backing condition affect the appropriate work.

Failed shower waterproofing and adjoining-room damp

Water appearing in a bedroom or below a tiled balcony may come from several sources. Plumbing, drainage, surface joints and the underlying wet-area system should be considered during assessment. Where deeper failure is identified, investigation and repairs may involve tile removal, substrate work, waterproofing and retiling. Those are distinct from surface maintenance and need an agreed scope; the visible stain alone cannot determine the complete repair.

What to ask before accepting a shower repair quote

Explain where water appears, when it happens and which repairs have already been attempted. Ask what has been assessed, what remains unknown and which surface or underlying work is included. Confirm how additional damage will be reported and approved. For apartments, include affected neighbouring areas and access arrangements. Avoid treating a maintenance quote as proof that every concealed component is sound.

Common Repair Questions

What is the difference between grout and silicone?

Grout and silicone serve different joint requirements in a tiled finish. Neither is a substitute for assessing the waterproofing beneath the tiles. The appropriate material and repair depend on the joint and condition.

How is resealing different from waterproofing a shower?

Resealing addresses specified surface joints. Waterproofing repairs address the underlying wet-area system and may involve removing and reinstating finishes. The required work follows assessment of the leak.

Why does a shower still leak after new silicone?

The leak may involve another joint, plumbing, movement or the underlying waterproofing. Report what was resealed and where damp persists. Repeating the same surface treatment without assessment may leave the cause unresolved.

Can replacing shower silicone fix every shower leak?

No. Replacing silicone only addresses the surface joint included in the work. A leaking shower may also have failed waterproofing, plumbing faults or movement beneath the tiles. Ellis assesses the symptoms before quoting resealing, regrouting or further investigation.

Can you assess black silicone around the shower base?

Yes. Explain whether there is staining only, split joints, loose tiles or damp in an adjoining room. We check the joint condition and signs of water entry before agreeing the surface repair. New silicone should not be offered as a guaranteed cure for an unidentified leak.

Cracked Floor Tiles & Loose Tile Repairs

Can just one cracked floor tile be replaced?

A local replacement may be possible where a suitable tile is available and the surrounding floor is sound. Ellis checks why it cracked and the supporting surface before quoting. Recurring cracks, movement or moisture should be investigated instead of repeatedly replacing a tile.

Can you assess a loose splashback tile behind the sink?

Yes. Tell us whether the tile has moved, grout is missing or the wall is damp. We inspect the tile and backing before agreeing a local repair. Leaking plumbing or damaged backing must be considered separately from the visible tile finish.

Failed Shower Waterproofing & Balcony Leak Assessment

When does a shower repair need tiles removed and waterproofing redone?

Tile removal and waterproofing repairs may be needed where assessment identifies failure beneath the surface. New silicone cannot establish that the underlying membrane is sound. Ellis confirms the investigation, repair sequence and scope before quoting the work.

Can you assess a shower leaking into the adjoining bedroom?

Yes. Describe where damp appears, when it occurs and whether previous resealing helped. The assessment considers plumbing, joints and the tiled wet-area system. Surface resealing, waterproofing and retiling are distinct works; the cause should be established before choosing the repair.
